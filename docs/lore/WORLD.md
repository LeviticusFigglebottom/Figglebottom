# Rimeholt: world bible

*Owner: narrative. Read with `SPEC.md`, `docs/art/STYLE.md`, `docs/lore/LEXICON.md` and `docs/lore/WRITING.md`. Every proper noun here is in the lexicon.*

This file states the truth plainly so the team agrees on it. **The game never does.** Nothing in section 3 or 4 is ever said aloud by anyone in Rimeholt. Each region lists *how the player finds out*: an object, a room, where an enemy stands, a line that stops halfway. If a fact has no delivery route, it is not in the game.

## 1. The land

Rimeholt is a highland of fjords and mountains running from the sea up to the ice. All its rivers start at one spring under the glacier, the **Coldwell**, and run down through the high pastures, the forest, past the great hall on its tarn, through the barrow hills and out to the fjords.

People give directions the way the water goes. *Up the water* is toward the Coldwell, the ice and the dead. *Down the water* is toward the sea, trade, and children sent away to be fostered.

There is no king. People live in **steadings**: one household, its hearth, its fields and boats or flock. A few steadings make a neighbourhood, and the neighbourhoods meet twice a year, at midsummer and at the Melt. The one office that spans the coast is the **Leadvoice**, who begins the Outsong at every burial. It is a job. The Leadvoice still mends her own roof.

## 2. Culture

### Households and hearth law

A household is whoever eats from one pot. Fosterlings, hired hands, an old neighbour taken in, a shipwrecked man who never got round to leaving. Three nights at the hearth and you are household, and the hearth answers for you.

Every child can say the hearth law:

1. The fire is not let out while one of the household lives. When the last one dies, the neighbours let it go on purpose, with a song.
2. A guest is household for three nights. After that they leave, or they are kin.
3. Quarrels are settled sitting side by side, facing the fire.
4. A child sent down the water carries a coal. It travels in a sealed fire-horn and is fed on the road. While it burns, the child still belongs to the hearth it came from.

Every hearth is set on one block of soapstone, the **hearthstone**, cut by the household's founders. People say it remembers whoever sat at it. They say it the way people say a house settles at night.

### Colours and masks

Each household has one colour, used sparingly: woad blue, madder red or lichen yellow, on a hem, a mask-band, a door-post. Saltfell's is woad blue.

Masks are carved by travelling **maskwrights**. Everyone gets a plain mask at coming of age, and the household carves a new mourning mask for every death. Masks are smooth: two eye holes, a brow line, the household knot cut small at the left temple. Every household cuts the brow differently, so a mask can be recognised from across a yard. People wear them at burials, at the Outsong, and when they go to fight.

Children have small masks too, hung on a peg by the door until they are needed. Most never are.

### The Outsong

The night after a burial the household sits at the hearth in mourning masks. The Leadvoice starts alone, then the household joins, then the neighbours standing outside the door. The song names the dead, tells them where the door is, and sends them up the water to wait under the Coldwell for the spring.

The masks are for the dead. People believe the dead turn back toward the face they loved best. If everyone is masked, the dead cannot find it, and they go. The masks are burned on the hearth before morning. People are not supposed to keep them. Some do.

At the **Melt**, the Coldwell breaks and the rivers run brown and loud, and the year's dead are carried down to the sea. People throw flowers into the water. It is bad luck to watch them out of sight, so everyone turns round at once and there is always someone who turns round too late and gets shouted at.

### Burial

The dead are washed, dressed in undyed wool and wrapped in their own cloak. Fisherfolk lie on the cliff above the strand, facing the water. Everyone else is carried to the **Lowe**, the barrow hills, where each neighbourhood has its kin-mounds. A barrow is opened, the dead are laid in, and the turf goes back the same day. One thing goes in with them, chosen by whoever knew them best: a net-needle, a whistle, a cup. Arguments about which thing are common and last for years.

### Seasons

- **Melt.** The Coldwell breaks. The dead go out. Lambing.
- **the Long Light.** Midsummer. The sun only dips. Fishing, hay, the neighbourhood feast. THAW is the last evening of the last Long Light.
- **Shearing.** The flocks come down from Shepherd's Reach.
- **Smoke-month.** Fish and mutton smoked, salt boiled, boats hauled up.
- **the Hearth-months.** The long dark. Carving, weaving, net-mending, the ice-fishing at Kettle Fjord.

### Trades

Fishing and net-mending on the coast; salt-boiling in iron pans. Turf-cutting and barrow-keeping on the Lowe. Ice-cutting and sledging on Kettle Fjord. Timber-wrighting and hall-keeping at Tarnhall. Charcoal-burning and hedge-laying in Thornwold. Herding on Shepherd's Reach. Smiths travel between neighbourhoods working bog-iron raked from the fens. Maskwrights travel too and never pay for a meal.

Nobody writes. Cargo, debts and songs are kept on **knot-cords**; tallies are notched sticks. Carved knots mark households. There are no letters anywhere in Rimeholt.

## 3. How the frost came (never stated in game)

The **coughing sickness** came ashore at Kettle Fjord in the Long Light, on a trader's boat. A dry cough that took the breath first. It took the old, the very young, and a few of the strong for no reason anyone could find.

The first death on the coast was **Wren**, the Leadvoice's youngest, five years old, at Saltfell. The Leadvoice was **Maren of Saltfell**, the Bearer's mother. They buried Wren on the cliff. The neighbours came to the door for the Outsong. Maren put on her mask and did not start.

She said, *tomorrow*. Nobody argued. The next night she said it again, and on the third night she took the mask off and went to bed.

The households took their lead from the Leadvoice, as they always had. When the next deaths came, at Saltfell and Kettle Fjord and Tarnhall, nobody wanted to be the first to sing when she had not. They kept the dead by the fire for one more night. Every hearth in Rimeholt burned high to keep someone warm who could not feel it, and the warmth of the world went into that.

Then Maren dug Wren up, wrapped her in her own cloak, and carried her up the water on foot. She meant to take her to the Coldwell herself, the long way, so she would have longer. She sat down at the wellhead with the child in her lap. The spring froze around them, and the frost came down every river in the middle of the summer and stayed.

Those who would not let go became **Holdfasts**, grown into the thing they kept holding. The unsung dead, and the living who froze sitting up beside them, are the **frost-taken**. They attack because the Bearer is walking toward the thing they are keeping.

## 4. The Bearer (assembled late, from pieces)

Years before the sickness, the Bearer was sent down the coast to foster with a boat-building household in the south. Maren gave them a coal from the long hearth in a horn and told them to keep it fed. Wren was a baby. The Bearer remembers carrying her on one hip round the yard, and nothing else about her.

Five years later the sickness came, and the trading boats stopped. No word came for fifteen more. The Bearer, grown, came home in a borrowed boat with the coal still alive. It is the last live coal in Rimeholt, because it is the only fire that was never spent keeping the dead warm.

What the player assembles, and in roughly what order:

1. **Saltfell.** A child's mask on the peg by the Longhouse door, woad-blue band, too small for anyone living. The player assumes it is the Bearer's own. Its item text never says. (It is Wren's. The Bearer's peg, next to it, is empty, because the Bearer took their mask south.)
2. **The Lowe.** One closed barrow on a road of open ones: the Bearer's father, who died of a winter fever years before the sickness and was sung out properly. On the turf someone has left a woad-blue thread tied to a stone. Fresh.
3. **Kettle Fjord.** Footprints frozen into the ice, one set, walking where the ice is thinnest, longer stride on the left as if carrying something on that hip.
4. **Tarnhall.** Outside the barred door, a scrap of woad wool caught on the latch at the height of a woman's hand. Nobody inside saw her.
5. **Thornwold.** A child's knucklebone dropped on the drove-road. The Bearer already owns the rest of the set (Wren's, found at Saltfell). This is the first point at which an attentive player knows who walked up the water.
6. **Shepherd's Reach.** Old Calder, in THAW, mentions "the Leadvoice's lass" coming up the fell last spring with a little one on her shoulders, singing something that was not a funeral song. He hums three notes of it. They are the notes of the Bearer's idle hum, the sound the Bearer makes when resting at a Hearthstone from the start of the game.
7. **The Coldwell.** The fostering horn, examined at the wellhead, gets a new description.

None of this is ever summarised by a character. The Bearer never speaks.

## 5. The regions

The Bearer travels up the water, sea to ice. For each Holdfast: who they were, what they keep doing, how they fight, and **what the player finds when they let go** (always an object or a changed room, never a speech).

### 5.1 Saltfell

The Bearer's home: a turf-and-stone shelf on a sea cliff above a shingle beach. Production detail in `docs/lore/SLICE.md`.

- **Areas:** *the Strand*, *Saltfell yard*, *the Longhouse*.
- **THAW:** boats drawn up, nets spread to dry, two children racing along the fish racks and being told off. Brede is checking his boat for an early start. Somebody coughs in the byre and somebody else starts talking louder.
- **RIME:** the tide froze mid-surge into a glass wall along the strand. Nets stand stiff as fences. The Longhouse door has iced shut from the inside.
- **Holdfast: the Netmender.** **Gunna of Saltfell**, Maren's older sister, the best net-mender on the coast and proud of it, which made her hard to live with. Her husband **Brede** rowed out at first light for the healer at Kettle Fjord. He came back three days later tangled in his own net. She cut him out, carried him home, and sat down to mend the hole in the net he had come back in. She has been mending that hole ever since. Every time she finishes it, she cuts it open again. In RIME she is a hunched figure wrapped in the frozen net, Brede bound against her back.
  - **Fights like:** casting and hauling. She throws the net across the floor and drags the Bearer in; she knots ice across the arena to close off ground; she turns her back, and the Bearer sees Brede's frozen face mask over her shoulder, and she will not let them near it.
  - **On release:** the net falls open and Brede slides down to lie by the long hearth. The player can pick up the **mending needle** from where she dropped it. Its text notes the net it was last used on has the same hole mended fourteen times, each knot slightly different, because she was getting tired.
- **Enemies:** Rimewalker, Oarsman, Hearthhound.
- **NPCs:** **Ingra**, steward of the Longhouse; **Brede**, alive in THAW.
- **Keepsakes:** Brede's net-float, the child-mask on the peg, Wren's knucklebones (all but one), a bowl at the long table set every night for someone who did not come.

### 5.2 The Lowe

Barrow hills behind the coast: long turf mounds in heather, a road of standing stones, peat cuttings full of black water.

- **Areas:** *the Barrow Road*, *the Kin-Mounds*, *the Open Barrow*.
- **THAW:** larks, turf stacked to dry, a family eating bread and cheese on a grandmother's mound because that is what you do at midsummer. Hesk the smith has his furnace going in a peat cutting and complains about the draught.
- **RIME:** every barrow on the Lowe stands open, turf doors propped on frozen spades. Inside, the dead sit up facing the road. One barrow is closed (see 4.2).
- **Holdfast: the Turfwright.** **Arne Holm**, who cut and laid barrow-turf for every neighbourhood on the coast and kept a notched stick for each, and could tell you who was under which mound by the cut of the turf. His twin daughters, **Liv and Asta**, died on the same night. He dug their barrow, laid them in, and stopped with the last turf on his spade. Since then he has cut turf. Stacks of it, hundreds of sods, enough to close every barrow on the Lowe, stacked neat around the one he will not close. In RIME his back has become the barrow's turf door, held open.
  - **Fights like:** heavy, patient work. He cuts the ground from under the Bearer, a line at a time. He stacks frozen sods into walls. When the Bearer gets behind him, he drops his back like a lid.
  - **On release:** the door comes down. The Open Barrow, revisited in THAW, now shows him sitting outside it with his daughters on a summer evening, arguing with them about which of them gets the window side of the barrow when they are old. His **tally-stick** has a notch for every barrow he closed, and two at the end that are cut halfway.
- **Enemies:** Barrow-sleeper (turf-wrapped dead, slow, hits hard), Keener (a masked mourner who lost the words and only breathes the tune; ranged), Cairn-carrier (frozen stones grown across its shoulders).
- **NPCs:** **Hesk**, travelling smith, the upgrade service from tier 2. **Aud**, a barrow-keeper's widow, who knows every mound and is wrong about two of them and will not be told.
- **Keepsakes:** the twins' spindles (one well-used, one never), Arne's tally-stick, the woad thread on the closed barrow.

### 5.3 Kettle Fjord

A deep fjord under sheer walls. It freezes in the Hearth-months and the fishing moves out onto the ice. The sickness came ashore here.

- **Areas:** *the Landing Ice*, *the Fish-Houses*, *the Breathing Hole*.
- **THAW:** open water on a warm evening, the big iron fish-kettles steaming, the trader's boat at the jetty with its sail half furled. One of its crew sits on a crate, coughing into his elbow, apologising to everybody.
- **RIME:** one sheet of ice wall to wall. Under a hand's depth of clear ice lie the boats that were trying to leave.
- **Holdfast: the Ice-Cutter.** **Old Pell**, who cut the fishing holes every winter and ran the sledge-dogs. His grandson **Ivo**, fourteen, took the sledge across the new ice to fetch the healer from the far shore without asking. Pell cut a hole where the tracks stopped and has kept it open, cutting it fresh every hour. Ivo's supper is still on the hook over the fish-house fire, and in RIME the pot has frozen with the ladle standing in it.
  - **Fights like:** breaking the floor. He cuts rings of ice that drop away; he drags the Bearer toward the hole on a frozen trace; he sends the dead dog team down the ice in a line.
  - **On release:** the hole skins over. Ivo's **mitten** is frozen into its new surface, palm up. The player can chip it free.
- **Enemies:** Ice-fisher (hands grown into ice-picks), Sledge-dog (fast, in pairs), Sealer (heavy, hooded, frozen club-arm).
- **NPCs:** **Ivo**, alive in THAW, harnessing the dogs and being told he is not going anywhere.
- **Keepsakes:** Ivo's mitten, the trader's cargo knot-cord (one knot added at the end in a different hand, recording "one man, sick"), a fish-kettle ladle.

### 5.4 Tarnhall

The great timber hall on a mountain tarn, where seven neighbourhoods came every Long Light to feast.

- **Areas:** *the Tarn Causeway*, *the Byres*, *the Long Table*.
- **THAW:** the feast at full roar. Benches packed, horns passed hand to hand, **Lusse** the hall-singer halfway through a very long song about a goat that ate a sail. Doors propped open for the air.
- **RIME:** doors barred from inside. The feast is still laid. The guests are in their seats, white, and some of them stand up when the Bearer comes in.
- **Holdfast: the Host.** **Gaute Haug**, keeper of the hall, host of the feast. He had bought the feast's fish at Kettle Fjord the week before, from the trader's boat. When the guests started coughing he barred the doors so nobody would carry it home, and once the dying started he could not open them, because opening them meant counting who was left. He kept filling cups. In RIME every horn on the table is full, frozen over, and set in front of someone who cannot drink it.
  - **Fights like:** a host who will not let a guest leave. He slides the long table across the hall to pin the Bearer; he throws benches; he raises the guests from their seats; he tips a horn and the floor freezes in a spreading pool.
  - **On release:** the bar lifts and the doors swing open by themselves. On the inside of the door-post is the guest-tally, knot after knot, one for each guest, which he kept retying because he kept losing count. The last few knots are the same knot, tied over and over.
- **Enemies:** Feast-guest (rises from a bench when passed), Cupbearer (quick, ice-blade hands), Hall-champion (board shield grown into its left forearm).
- **NPCs:** **Lusse**, hall-singer, who in THAW teaches songs and gets the Bearer to join in the goat song's chorus whether they like it or not.
- **Keepsakes:** the guest-tally, Lusse's goat song (a Song), the Host's own cup, which is the only empty one.

### 5.5 Thornwold

Old birch and pine, coppice, charcoal clearings, laid hedges along the drove-roads.

- **Areas:** *the Collier's Clearing*, *the Hedge*.
- **THAW:** charcoal clamps smoking under turf, pollen thick in the slanting light, two young people arguing about where a gate should go, both enjoying it.
- **RIME:** every hedge has grown into a wall of frozen thorn, and the drove-road has become a maze that ends at one grave under the oldest pine.
- **Holdfast: the Hedgelayer.** **Eyvind Brask**, hedge-layer, betrothed to **Linnet**, a collier. She died at the clearing. He buried her under the pine and started laying a hedge round the grave so nobody could come and sing her out. He laid it well, because he was good at his work, and then he laid another round that, and another. There is a gap in the innermost ring where a gate should be. He never decided where it went. In RIME he is a lean figure of woven, laid thorn-stems, mask half grown over with briar.
  - **Fights like:** building. Thorn walls rise across the arena to cut the Bearer off, then close in. He steps into the hedge and comes out somewhere else.
  - **On release:** the hedge unlays itself stem by stem. At the grave is Linnet's **song-cord**, a knotted cord: she tied her own Outsong the night before she died, so someone could sing it for her. Eyvind never learned to read song-knots. Lusse could have. The player, by this point, has met Lusse.
- **Enemies:** Collier (slow, ember-black frozen hands), Rime-stag (red deer frozen mid-bound, thorn grown through its antlers).
- **NPCs:** **Linnet**, alive in THAW, black to the elbows, cheerful, winning the gate argument.
- **Keepsakes:** Linnet's song-cord, a hedging bill, a betrothal ring of plaited birch, and Wren's missing knucklebone on the drove-road (see 4.5).

### 5.6 Shepherd's Reach

High pasture under the glacier: stone sheilings, folds, scree and sky.

- **Areas:** *the Sheilings*, *the High Fold*.
- **THAW:** the flocks on the high grass. A woman calls them in with a long rising cry and every sheep on the mountain looks up.
- **RIME:** the flocks froze where they were when she last called. A whole slope of white sheep, all facing the High Fold.
- **Holdfast: the Fellcaller.** **Sigga Calder**, whose calls carried three valleys. Her father, **Old Calder**, caught the cough and walked up to the high fold in the night so she would not catch it from him. He left the door of the sheiling on the latch and his boots by it, because he was not coming back for them. She followed his bare footprints and found him. She has been calling the flock to him ever since, as though he would want to count them.
  - **Fights like:** calls. A rising cry that staggers; a herd summoned in a rushing line; a head-down charge under the ram's horns that frame her mask.
  - **On release:** one soft call, and the whole slope of sheep lies down at once. In the High Fold, Old Calder's **fold-tally** is there with every ewe's notch, including one he cut for Sigga as a joke when she was small, because she always came when called.
- **Enemies:** Frost-ram (a charging quadruped), Drover (long-limbed, a crook of ice grown from both hands).
- **NPCs:** **Old Calder**, alive in THAW, mending a fold wall and talking to the sheep by name (see 4.6).
- **Keepsakes:** the shepherd's whistle, the fold-tally, Old Calder's boots, still by the sheiling door.

### 5.7 The Coldwell

The glacier's source. A frozen river climbs through ice-falls to a ring of old stones round the spring under the glacier's lip.

- **Areas:** *the Upwater*, *the Wellhead*.
- **THAW:** the only THAW in the game that is not the last midsummer evening. It is the last Melt before the sickness, the spring Maren brought Wren up to see the water go. The water is loud and brown with flowers turning in it, and a woman with a woad-blue mask-band is holding a small child's hand at the edge and telling the child not to watch the flowers out of sight. The child watches anyway.
- **RIME:** every unsung dead of Rimeholt stands in the frozen river, facing up the water, in no hurry.
- **Holdfast: the Leadvoice.** **Maren of Saltfell**. She sits at the wellhead, grown huge, her cloak spread into the ice of the spring, Wren against her chest in a swaddle of clear ice bound with a woad-blue band.
  - **Fights like:** a voice that will not start. She does not reach for the Bearer much. She holds long notes that freeze the air between them. The unsung dead come up the river and stand in the way. In the last phase she stands up for the first time, and the fight becomes a matter of getting close enough to sing.
  - **On release:** the Bearer sings Wren out. The swaddle clears, the band comes loose, and the player picks it up. Then the choice.
- **Enemies:** Upwalker (one of the dead, half-climbed, slow), the Unsung (thin clear-ice figures that break when sung at).
- **NPCs:** **Maren** and **Wren**, in THAW at the remembered Melt. Maren talks to the Bearer like a friendly stranger and asks if they have far to go.
- **Keepsakes:** Wren's band, Maren's mask, and the fostering horn's last description: *"The horn a child carries down the water. The coal inside has been fed for twenty years. It was given with an instruction, and the instruction was followed."*

## 6. The endings

**The Outsong.** The Bearer puts on Maren's mask and sings. Then the Bearer opens the horn and lays the coal in the spring. The Coldwell breaks and every river runs. Last shot: the Strand at Saltfell in a real spring, grey and green and empty, two masks on the Longhouse peg. Every Hearthstone in the game now shows only the present. THAW cannot be returned to.

**The Hearth Kept.** The Bearer keeps the horn shut and sits down at the wellhead. The Melt goes on in THAW: loud brown water, flowers, a woman holding a child's hand. Credits roll over it with no music, only the river.

Neither ending is commented on.
